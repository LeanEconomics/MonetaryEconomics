# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapter 5 of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of the
Price Level*, or in the Online Appendix sections it uses (§A1.3, §A1.7, §§A1.9–A1.13 and §A2), is
false, imprecise, or relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs from
the printed one. In each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the original.

Codes E19–E54 are the Chapter 5 and Online Appendix items of the survey of Chapters 4–5
(`notes/survey_cochrane_ch4-5.md`); E10 is that survey's Chapter 4 item about the surplus rule
used in Chapter 5. "E6 (Chapters 11–19)" is the `γ` condition of the survey of Chapters 11–19
(`notes/survey_cochrane_ch11-19.md`), which the survey flags for (5.37). Each was confirmed or
refuted by formalisation before being recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation
shows the finding itself to be wrong: E20 and E32 are refined below. Five findings are not in the
survey and were found during formalisation: the sign in (5.41), the role of persistence on
p. 131, the output claim on p. 123, the cumulative-inflation formula on p. 167, and the terminal
condition behind (5.3). Several claims that the book asserts without proof turned out to be
correct; they are listed in the last section.

Out of scope are the verbal parts: most of §5.1.3 (pp. 125–127), §5.3 beyond its precise clauses
(pp. 130–133), §5.4.3 (pp. 143–145), the shock definitions of §5.5.6 (pp. 159–160), the
interest-cost surplus rule of pp. 161–162, and the review §5.8 (pp. 181–186). Numbers that need a
full numerical solution (most of Table 5.1, Figs. 5.6–5.9 and 5.14–5.15) are not checked. Lean
names are relative to the namespace `Cochrane.StickyPriceFiscal`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (5.38), p. 139 (E33) | For `s̃ = αv* + γ(v − v*) + u`, "the condition for active fiscal policy is `γ = 0`, not `α = 0`." | The explosive root `ρ⁻¹` of `v − v*` loads on actual debt with weight `(α − γ)/α`: `ρ^TE_tv_{t+T} → (α − γ)(v_t − v*_t)/α` (`ActivePassive.tvc_v_iff_538`). So policy is passive iff `γ = α` and active iff `γ ≠ α`, whenever there is a genuine mean-zero shock (`ActivePassive.active_538`, `ActivePassive.passive_538`, `ActivePassive.active_iff_538`). On the coin-flip tree with `α = 1/10`, the rule with `γ = 1/5 ≠ 0` is active (`ActivePassive.printed_538_false`). `γ = 0` is sufficient, not necessary. |
| (5.41), p. 139 (found during formalisation) | `ρv_{t+1} = v_t − β_sε_{t+1} − s̃_{t+1}`. | The sign of `β_sε` is wrong: (5.31) and (5.33) give `+β_sε_{t+1}`, as in (5.23) and App. (2.2) (`ActivePassive.Reduced`, `ActivePassive.reduced_of_model`). In equilibrium the printed equation holds iff `β_sε_{t+1} = 0` (`ActivePassive.printed_541_sign`). |
| p. 131 (found during formalisation) | "For an interest-rate rise to lower inflation … the interest-rate rise must be persistent and unexpected." | Persistence is not needed for the sign. With `ρ = β`, as in the book's calibrations, an unexpected AR(1) rise `i_t = η^t` of any persistence `0 ≤ η < 1`, including a one-period rise, lowers inflation on impact iff `ω̃ > ρ/λ₁`, the same condition as for a permanent rise (`LongTermDebt.ar1_disinflation_iff_rho_eq_beta`, `LongTermDebt.ltd_disinflation_iff`). Persistence only enlarges the disinflation (`LongTermDebt.ar1_magnitude_rho_eq_beta`). Long-term debt is necessary (`LongTermDebt.ar1_short_debt_no_disinflation`, `HigherOrLower.needs_long_debt`), and so is surprise (`HigherOrLower.anticipated_rise_no_disinflation`). For `ρ ≠ β` the exact impact response is the closed form `LongTermDebt.ar1_impact`. |

The sentence holds only with flexible prices (`TwoSidedMA.flex_one_period_after`). The closed form of the anticipated rise, `FiscalNK.anticipated_rise`, has the forward term `Bλ₂^{S−t}` before the rise. |

| p. 121; pp. 136–137, 148 (E32, refined) | "`ρ ≤ 1` provides the additional forward-looking root"; "the condition that debt cannot explode means `v = v*`"; "this equation has a forward-looking or unit root. Therefore the unique stationary equilibrium …". | For `0 < ρ < 1` boundedness selects: the model (5.15)–(5.16) is determinate (`FiscalNK.fiscalNK_determinate`), and `v = v*` (`ActivePassive.select_bounded`, `PolicyRules.full_elim`). At `ρ = 1` it does not: every bounded equilibrium of (5.15)–(5.16) has bounded companions (`FiscalNK.rho_one_not_determinate`), and on the coin-flip tree a bounded solution of (5.29)–(5.34) has `v ≠ v*` (`ActivePassive.rho_one_counterexample`; also `PolicyRules.rho_one_not_elim`). The transversality condition selects at every `ρ` (`FiscalNK.rho_one_tvc_unique`, `FiscalNK.eqm_unique_tvc`, `ActivePassive.select_tvc`). The survey's "strict stationarity" is not formalised: there is no stationarity notion for an abstract `ExpectationSystem`. |
| App. pp. 574–576 (E20, refined) | "We need two eigenvalues greater or equal to one"; "`λ_k ≥ 1` solved forward". | A unit eigenvalue is not a forward-looking one. In the Blanchard–Kahn sense, for some bounded forcing, the `(x, π)` block on the boundary `κ(θ_iπ − 1) + (1 − β)θ_ix = 0` is not determinate (`PolicyRules.block_not_determinate_of_boundary`, `PolicyRules.appA1_19_unit`, `BKCopy.GeneralBK.not_determinate_of_unit`). At the level of the model, with its own stationary AR(1) forcing, the answer depends on the information structure: at most one equilibrium always (`PolicyRules.unitroot_unique`); existence iff the inflation martingale is bounded (`PolicyRules.unitroot_exists_iff`); determinate under perfect foresight (`PolicyRules.unitroot_pf`); determinate with recurring news iff `β_s = 0` and `β_i = k` (`PolicyRules.unitroot_iff_trunc`). So "≥ 1" is wrong in general, but the survey's reading "a unit eigenvalue destroys determinacy" holds only in the Blanchard–Kahn sense, not for the model's own forcing under perfect foresight. |
| pp. 135, 137–138 (E31) and §5.5, p. 148 | `a(ρ) = β_s` and "`v*` is stationary for `α > 1 − ρ`"; the model with rules has "the unique stationary equilibrium". | Upper bounds are missing. `a(ρ) = β_s` needs `0 < α < 2` (`SurplusProcess.hasSum_aCoef`), and without it the series need not exist (`SurplusProcess.aCoef_not_summable`). `v*` is bounded iff `1 − ρ < α < 1 + ρ` (`ActivePassive.vstar_bounded`, `ActivePassive.vstar_unbounded`), and `ρ^Tv*_T → 0` needs `α < 2` (`ActivePassive.vstar_discounted`, `ActivePassive.vstar_discounted_fails`). For `\|1 − α\| > ρ` there is no equilibrium for some initial debt (`PolicyRules.nonexist_of_alpha`). |
| p. 138 (E54; E6 of Chapters 11–19) | "For `γ > 1 − ρ`, debt converges going forward for any value of unexpected inflation"; "for `γ > 0` … the transversality condition holds". | Each needs an upper bound. The transversality condition holds for every inflation process iff `0 < γ < 2` (`ActivePassive.gamma_passive_iff`, `ActivePassive.gamma_tvc`). Debt stays bounded for `1 − ρ < γ < 1 + ρ` (`ActivePassive.gamma_bounded`) and explodes for `\|1 − γ\| > ρ` (`ActivePassive.gamma_unbounded`). |
| Ch. 4, p. 96 (E10) | `s̃_{t+1} = αv_t + u_{t+1}` "is compatible with active fiscal policy". | A rule on actual debt is passive for `0 < α < 2` (`ActivePassive.e10_actual_debt_rule_passive`); the active version responds to `v*` (`ActivePassive.active_538`). |
