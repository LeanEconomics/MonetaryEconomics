# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapters 17–18 (with Online Appendix A1, §1.8 and
§1.13.1) of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level*, is false, imprecise, or
relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs from the printed one. In
each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the original.

Codes E7–E11 and E24 are the Chapter 17 items of the survey of Chapters 11–19
(`notes/survey_cochrane_ch11-19.md`). Each was confirmed or refuted by formalisation before being
recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be wrong. The
survey marked E8 and E24 as suspected: E8 is confirmed, and E24 (the `Δs` values of Figure 17.6)
is resolved into a new correction. Three further errors are not in the survey: the shock matrix of
A1 (1.55), the dating of the real rate in (17.19), and the labels of Figure 17.6. Several claims
that the book asserts without proof turned out to be correct; they are listed in the last section.

Out of scope are the verbal passages: the history of thought of Chapter 18, Sims' (2016)
backward-looking model (p. 431), the determinacy plots of Cochrane (2011b) (p. 420, A1 p. 587),
and the remarks on why real explosions are not observed (p. 421). Lean names are relative to the
namespace `Cochrane.KeynesianStickyPrices`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p. 402 (E7), adaptive expectations | (17.10): "The Taylor rule `\|φ\| > 1` stabilizes" inflation. | The coefficient on lagged inflation is `(1 + σκ)/(1 + σκφ)`, and the model is stable iff `\|1 + σκ\| < \|1 + σκφ\|`, i.e. iff **`φ > 1` or `φ < −1 − 2/(σκ)`** (`RulesAdaptiveRE.adaptiveStable_iff`, `RulesAdaptiveRE.abs_lt_abs_iff`). For `−1 − 2/(σκ) < φ < −1` the claim fails: with `σ = 1`, `κ = 1/2`, `φ = −3/2` the coefficient is 6 and inflation spirals away (`RulesAdaptiveRE.printed_abs_phi_gt_one_stabilizes_false`). At `φ = −1 − 2/(σκ)` the coefficient is exactly `−1` and inflation oscillates forever (`RulesAdaptiveRE.boundary_coeff`). The companion claim "`\|φ\| < 1` produces unstable inflation" is true (`RulesAdaptiveRE.passive_unstable`). |
| p. 402 (E7), rational expectations | (17.11): "passive policy `φ < 1` produces stable, indeterminate inflation". | (17.11) is determinate iff **`φ > 1` or `φ < −1 − 2/(σκ)`** (`RulesAdaptiveRE.reDeterminate_iff`); the coefficient `(1 + σκφ)/(1 + σκ)` has modulus below one iff `−1 − 2/(σκ) < φ < 1` (`RulesAdaptiveRE.re_coeff_abs_lt_one_iff`). With `σ = 1`, `κ = 1/2`, `φ = −6 < 1` the model is determinate (`RulesAdaptiveRE.printed_passive_indeterminate_false`). On `[−1 − 2/(σκ), 1]`, in particular for passive `0 ≤ φ ≤ 1`, it is indeterminate (`RulesAdaptiveRE.passive_indeterminate`). The full model's bound (1.58) reduces to this one at `β = 0` (`Links.determinate_beta_zero_iff`). |
| p. 405 (E8) | The inverse of (17.14): `E_tπ*_{t+1} = σκ/(1 + σκ)∑_{j≥0}(1 + σκ)^{−j}i*_{t−j}`. | Past expectational errors are omitted. On an infinite past, with `δ_s` the expectational errors, `E_tπ*_{t+1} = σκ/(1 + σκ)∑_{j≥0}(1 + σκ)^{−j}i*_{t−j} + ∑_{j≥1}(1 + σκ)^{−j}δ_{t+1−j}` (`PersistenceFreedom.corrected_inverse`). The printed form holds when there were no past surprises (`PersistenceFreedom.printed_inverse_of_no_surprise`) and fails for a single surprise `δ_1 = 1`, a dashed equilibrium of Figure 17.2 (`PersistenceFreedom.printed_inverse_false`). |
| p. 418 (E9) | With `x* = 0`, `i*_t − φπ*_t = (1 − φ)E_t∑βʲu_{π,t+1+j} + u_{x,t}/σ`. | The intercept is `(1 − φβ)E_t∑βʲu_{π,t+1+j} − φu_{π,t} + u_{x,t}/σ` (`OptimalPolicy.e9_intercept`). The two differ by `φ[(1 − β)E_t∑βʲu_{π,t+1+j} − u_{π,t}]` (`OptimalPolicy.e9_difference`). Counterexample: under perfect foresight with `u_{π,t} = 0.7ᵗ`, `u_x = 0`, `β = 0.95`, `φ = 1.5` the date-0 intercept is `−160/67 ≈ −2.388`, against the printed `−70/67 ≈ −1.045` (`OptimalPolicy.e9_counterexample`). |
| p. 414 (E10) | "5 × 2.23 = 11.5 percent of GDP". | `5 × 2.23 = 11.15` (`FiscalUnderpinnings.e10`). At $200 billion per point of GDP (p. 411) this is $2.23 trillion, which matches the printed "$2.2 trillion", so only the percentage is a slip. |
| A1 (1.55), p. 586 (new) | The shock matrix of `E_tx_{t+1}`: `β⁻¹[−1, σ, σβ](u_x, u_π, u_i)'`. | The `u_x` entry inside the `β⁻¹` is `−β`, not `−1`: `E_tx_{t+1} = β⁻¹[(β + σκ)x_t − σ(1 − βφ)π_t] + β⁻¹[−βu_{x,t} + σu_{π,t} + σβu_{i,t}]` (`TaylorDeterminacy.eq_1_55`). The printed first row holds at a date and state iff `β = 1` or `u_{x,t} = 0` there (`TaylorDeterminacy.printed_1_55_iff`). The transition matrix itself is right ((17.33), `TaylorDeterminacy.deviation_iff_17_33`). |
| Fig. 17.6, p. 410 (new; resolves E24) | Equilibrium A has `δ₁ = 1%` and `Δs = −1.53`, equilibrium E has `δ₁ = −1%` and `Δs = 2.99`, alongside D (`π₁ = 0`, `Δs = 1.66`) and `δ = 0` (`Δs = 0.67`). | The surplus change is affine in initial inflation for every parameter value: `Δs(p) = K(1 − p) − p` with `K = 1/(λ₁ − ρ)`, or `ρ/(λ₁ − ρ)` with (17.16)'s dating (`FiscalUnderpinnings.deltaS_closed`). Given the printed D and `δ = 0` values, each up to rounding, a jump of `+1` gives `Δs < −1.9` and a jump of `−1` gives `Δs > 3.2` (`FiscalUnderpinnings.fig17_6_inconsistent`). So the labels and the numbers for A and E cannot both be right, whatever the parameters. The printed `Δs` values correspond to jumps in `(0.82, 0.84)` and `(−0.88, −0.86)` relative to the `δ = 0` equilibrium (`FiscalUnderpinnings.fig17_6_implied_jumps`). |

## Claims that need a changed statement

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 §1.8, p. 572 (E11) | "`φ > 1` so both roots are unstable, `λ₁ < 1` and `λ₂ < 1`" (lag roots). | The roots can be complex: with `φ = 1.5`, `β = 0.99`, `σ = 1`, `κ = 0.5` the discriminant is negative (`AR1Responses.lag_roots_complex_example`). The correct statement is about moduli: for `φ > 1` both roots have modulus below one (`AR1Responses.lag_roots_norm_lt_one`), and the lag-operator solution equals (1.60) with complex roots (`AR1Responses.lag_form`, `AR1Responses.lag_factor`). |
| p. 420; A1 p. 587 (E11) | "`φ > 1` generates two explosive (`λ > 1`) eigenvalues"; "if `φ > 1` then both eigenvalues are greater than one". | For `φ > 1` the eigenvalues of (1.57) are either real and above one or complex with modulus above one (`TaylorDeterminacy.roots_phi_gt_one`, `Links.eigen_norm_gt_one`); the example above is in the complex branch (`Links.complex_example_discE`). The printed formula (1.57) is right where the discriminant is nonnegative (`TaylorDeterminacy.eq_1_57`). |
| A1 p. 588 (part of E11) | "the inflation response (1.62) is a natural generalization of" (17.12) and (16.9). | The inflation response is (1.60); (1.62) is the interest rate (`AR1Responses.msv_i_eq`). The generalisation is proved for (1.60): at `β = 0` it is (17.12), and as `κ → ∞` it tends to (16.9) (`AR1Responses.msv_beta_zero`, `AR1Responses.tendsto_1_60`). |
| (17.19), p. 411 (new) | `ΔE_{t+1}π_{t+1} = −ΔE_{t+1}∑ρʲs̃_{t+1+j} + ΔE_{t+1}∑ρʲr_{t+1+j}`, with the text reading `r_{t+1}` as the ex-post rate `i_t − π_{t+1}`. | The printed equation is exact for every `ρ` when `r_{t+1+j}` is the ex-ante real rate earned between `t + j` and `t + 1 + j`, `i_{t+j} − E_{t+j}π_{t+1+j}` (`FiscalUnderpinnings.eq_17_19`, `FiscalUnderpinnings.eq_17_19_bounded`). With the dating of (17.16), `r_t = i_t − E_tπ_{t+1}`, the real-rate sum carries an extra factor `ρ` (`FiscalUnderpinnings.eq_17_19_own_dating`), and the printed form holds iff `ρ = 1` or there is no real-rate news (`FiscalUnderpinnings.printed_own_dating_iff`). With the ex-post reading the `j = 0` term is `−ΔE_{t+1}π_{t+1}` itself (`FiscalUnderpinnings.expost_first_term`), so that reading is wrong. Figure 17.7 uses `ρ = 1`, so its numbers are unaffected. |
| A1 p. 587 | "with `φ < 1` we have multiple equilibria". | False below the bound (1.58), as the book's next paragraph says: with `φ_x = 0` the model is determinate iff `φ > 1` or `φ < −(1 + 2(1 + β)/(σκ))` (`TaylorDeterminacy.determinate_iff_phi`); for `φ < 1` the stable eigenvalue lies in `(−1, 1)` exactly above that bound (`TaylorDeterminacy.roots_phi_lt_one`). |
| p. 420; A1 p. 587; Ch. 16 p. 369; Ch. 12 p. 328 | "In general `φ > 1` is neither necessary nor sufficient for explosive eigenvalues. Adding `φ_x x_t` responses widens the range". | Non-necessity holds even with `φ_x = 0`, through the oscillating branch (1.58) (`TaylorDeterminacy.not_necessary_negative`), and with `φ_x > 0` every `φ ∈ [0, 1)` can be made determinate (`TaylorDeterminacy.not_necessary_witness`, `TaylorDeterminacy.not_necessary_phix`). Non-sufficiency **needs `φ_x < 0`**: with `φ_x ≥ 0`, `φ > 1` is sufficient (`TaylorDeterminacy.sufficient_of_phix_nonneg`; for `φ, φ_x ≥ 0` the region is `κ(φ − 1) + (1 − β)φ_x > 0`, `TaylorDeterminacy.determinate_iff_BM`), while every `φ` is made indeterminate by `φ_x = −(1 + σκφ)/σ` (`TaylorDeterminacy.not_sufficient`, `TaylorDeterminacy.not_sufficient_witness`). Raising a nonnegative output response preserves determinacy (`TaylorDeterminacy.widens_with_phix`). |
| p. 428 | Clarida, Galí and Gertler: "`φ_π < 1` means multiple-equilibrium volatility, and `φ_π > 1` means determinacy". | With an output response `φ_x ≥ 0`, `φ_π > 1` does mean determinacy (`History.rule181_phi_gt_one`), but for `φ_π ∈ [0, 1)` the equilibrium is indeterminate only if `(1 − β)φ_x ≤ κ(1 − φ_π)` (`History.cgg_reading`). |
| Fig. 17.5, p. 409 | "Output declines." | Made precise: along the no-surprise response output is negative at every date up to and including the date of the rise (`FullModel.antPath_x_neg`), because output is low exactly when `π_t < βπ_{t+1}` (`FullModel.x_neg_iff`); it converges to `(1 − β)/κ`, not to zero (`FullModel.antPath_x_tendsto`). Before the rise inflation grows at rate `1/λ₂` (`FullModel.antPath_le_T`). |
| Fig. 17.5, p. 409 | The solid line: "the response to a fully-expected interest-rate rise". | Read as a rise known from the infinite past (the figure has no announcement date). On ℤ there is exactly one bounded path, `FullModel.zPath` (`FullModel.zPath_existsUnique`). It coincides with the response announced at any earlier date from the announcement on, and every response converges to it as the announcement recedes (`FullModel.zPath_eq_antPath`, `FullModel.tendsto_zPath`). Existence on ℤ is proved for the step only; uniqueness for every bounded rate path (`FullModel.secondZ_unique`). |
| (17.8), p. 400 | The bounded solutions of (17.7) are the backward moving average (17.8). | Proved in two forms: with a finite start, a closed form including `π_0` (`StaticIS.reFamily_closed`); with an infinite past, pathwise on ℤ, every bounded solution has the form (17.8) and conversely (`StaticIS.eq_17_8`, `SharedTools.backward_series_solves`). |
| Fig. 17.6, p. 410 (E24) | The `Δs` numbers, "described in more detail in Cochrane (2017b)". | Defined from (17.19) with `0 ≤ ρ < λ₁` as the percentage change in steady-state surpluses (`FiscalUnderpinnings.deltaS`, `FiscalUnderpinnings.permanent_surplus_pct`). B has `Δs = −1` for every parameter value, matching the printed −1.00 (`FiscalUnderpinnings.deltaS_B`); D has `Δs = 1/(λ₁ − ρ) > 0`, and C is not the `ΔE₁π₁ = 0` equilibrium (`FiscalUnderpinnings.deltaS_D`, `FiscalUnderpinnings.deltaS_zero_iff`). The book gives no `σ, κ, β, ρ` for the figure, so 0.67, 1.66, −1.53 and 2.99 cannot be recomputed. |
| p. 405 | "Any value of unexpected inflation is consistent with any persistence of the interest-rate target." | Stated for news `ε` revealed at date 1 (`E_0ε = 0`, `E_1ε = ε`): for every bounded rate path and every `c` there is a bounded equilibrium with `ΔE_1π*_1 = cε` (`PersistenceFreedom.any_sign`), also on the coin-flip tree (`PersistenceFreedom.any_sign_coin`), and in the full model under perfect foresight after the announcement (`PersistenceFreedom.full_any_sign`). |
| p. 406 | The long-run response of inflation to interest rates is always positive. | Proved along any history in which the rate converges and the expectational errors die out: inflation converges to the rate (`PersistenceFreedom.long_run_one`); in the full model for every bounded equilibrium (`FullModel.tendsto_pi`). |
| p. 419 | "different values of `φ` without a time-varying intercept cannot fully stabilize inflation or output". | Proved for the rule `i_t = φπ_t + c`: zero inflation pins the IS disturbance whatever `φ` (`OptimalPolicy.pi_zero_constant_intercept`), so with no IS shock and a geometric cost-push shock no `(φ, c)` works (`OptimalPolicy.no_rule_stabilises_inflation`); zero output with an AR(1) cost-push shock forces `φ = ρ < 1` and `c = 0` (`OptimalPolicy.x_zero_constant_intercept`). |
| p. 421 | "nominal explosions can also be real explosions". | Proved for `φ < 1/β`, in particular for `1 < φ < 1/β`: a non-selected path of the deviation system has `βᵗx̃_t → −∞` (`TaylorDeterminacy.real_explosion`). |
| (18.3), p. 427 | "write it King's way, and the likelihood function will be flat". | Proved for any function of the equilibrium paths, not for a likelihood of a probability model: it takes the same value for all `φ` in the determinacy region (`History.likelihood_flat`). |
| p. 416 (rounding only) | "η = 0.51". | `0.508 < η* < 0.509` at the Figure 17.8 parameters (`AR1Responses.etaStar_fig17_8`). |
