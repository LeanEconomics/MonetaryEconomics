# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapters 8–10 of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of
the Price Level*, or in the two later claims formalised here (§11.5, p. 322, and §24.3, p. 527),
is false, imprecise, or relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs
from the printed one. In each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the
original.

Codes E8.1–E8.4, E9.1–E9.10 and E10.1–E10.4 are the Chapter 8–10 items of the survey of Chapters
6–10 (`notes/survey_cochrane_ch6-10.md`). Each was confirmed or refuted by formalisation before
being recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be
wrong. E9.9 was the survey's open question for these chapters; it is now settled (first
section). E8.5, an index slip in (8.4), is not in the survey and was found during formalisation.
E10.3 turned out not to be an error, and E9.6 and E9.10 were checked correct; they are listed in
the last section with the other claims that the book asserts without proof and that turned out
to be correct.

Out of scope are the institutional and historical passages: §8.2's interpretation of pegs and the
gold standard, the corporate-finance and moral-hazard discussion of §§8.3–8.4, most of §8.6, §9.1,
the verbal parts of §9.6, and §§10.2–10.7 beyond the balance-sheet identities. Lean names are
relative to the namespace `Cochrane.AssetsRulesBalanceSheets`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §9.3.2, p. 267 (E9.9) | With the dynamic fiscal rule and indexed debt, and the interest-rate target (9.12), "only the sequence `P_t = P*_t` satisfies both (9.9) and (9.12)". | **False under uncertainty in general.** An explicit infinite settle-or-continue tree is an equilibrium for every `0 < β < 1`, every `0 < P_0 < 1 = P*_0`, every `θ` and every initial debt `b̄` equal to the present value of `s₀`: it has genuine probabilities in `(0, 1)`, satisfies (9.12) at every node, (9.8) on every edge with predetermined real debt, (9.9) at every node as a genuine convergent series of conditional expectations and ex post along every settling path, and the transversality condition (`FiscalRules.Sunspot.sunspot_counterexample`, `FiscalRules.Sunspot.valuation_expost`). **True under perfect foresight** (`FiscalRules.fr_perfectForesight`, `FiscalRules.fr_exists`). In general only `P_0 ≤ P*_0` survives, by Jensen's inequality (`FiscalRules.fr_jensen`). **Settled:** under any expectation system with the pull-out property, with `P* ≡ 1`, predetermined real debt, initial debt equal to the present value of `s₀` and the transversality condition, a price level that is **bounded above, or bounded away from zero**, satisfies (9.9) and (9.12) only if `P ≡ P*` (`FiscalRules.fr_unique_bounded`, via `FiscalRules.unique_of_ratio_tvc`). The counterexample's prices are unbounded above and tend to zero (`FiscalRules.Sunspot.settlePrice_unbounded`, `FiscalRules.Sunspot.contPrice_tendsto_zero`), so it violates both bounds. Corrected statement: add "among price paths bounded above, or bounded away from zero". |
| Bullet, p. 280 (E9.8) | "Offering to freely exchange real debt for nominal debt at the rate `P*`" drives the expected price level to `E_t(1/P_{t+1}) = 1/P*`, and the opportunity disappears. | It does not, with surpluses and the initial sales held fixed. The book's own differential `de/dB = −(e − 1/P*)/B` (`SpreadSwap.hasDerivAt_eSwap`) integrates to the hyperbola `e(B) − 1/P* = C/B` with `C = B₀(e₀ − 1/P*)` (`SpreadSwap.eSwap_sub_target`, `SpreadSwap.gap_const`). The target is reached at a finite `B` iff the gap was zero to begin with (`SpreadSwap.swap_reaches_target_iff`). When the public's real debt is exhausted the gap is still `C/(B₀ + P*b₀)` (`SpreadSwap.real_debt_exhausted`); in the survey's example it falls only from `7/30` to `35/222` (`SpreadSwap.numeric_example`). The direction is right (`SpreadSwap.eSwap_strictAntiOn`, `SpreadSwap.eSwap_gt_target`). Corrected statement: swaps at `P*` leave `b + B/P*` unchanged, and the target holds iff `β(b + B/P*) = W`, which a passive supply of nominal debt sold for goods reaches at `B = P*(W/β − b)` (`SpreadSwap.swap_invariant`, `SpreadSwap.passive_supply`). |
| (9.26), p. 277 (E9.4) | `σr_t = +ΔE_tπ_t/κ + u_{x,t} + u_{π,t} − E_tu_{π,t+1}`. | The correct formula is `σr_t = −ΔE_tπ_t/κ + u_{x,t} + (u_{π,t} − E_tu_{π,t+1})/κ` (`SpreadTarget.r_formula`); the difference is `SpreadTarget.printed_minus_corrected`. The missing `1/κ` is refuted under perfect foresight (`SpreadTarget.printed_9_26_fails_perfectForesight`) and the sign in the coin-flip tree (`SpreadTarget.printed_9_26_fails_coin`). |
| p. 296 (E10.1) | With the perpetuity as numeraire, `P_t = (1 + r)y/((1 + y)r)`. | (10.7) gives `P_t = (1 + y)r/((1 + r)y)` (`SharesAsMoney.perpetuity_price_level`, `SharesAsMoney.perpetuity_price_level_process`); the printed formula is its reciprocal (`SharesAsMoney.printed_reciprocal`), and the two agree iff `y = r` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_eq_iff`): at `r = .04`, `y = .05` they are `21/26` and `26/21` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_counterexample`). The book's conclusion "define `y = r` so `P_t = 1`" stands (`SharesAsMoney.price_level_one_iff`). |
| p. 296 (E10.2) | With one-period indexed bonds as numeraire, "`P_t = β`", and each maturing bond is redeemed for `β` new bonds. | A bond is worth `β` goods (`SharesAsMoney.oneperiod_real_value`), so the price level is `P_t = 1/β` and each maturing bond is redeemed for `1/β` new bonds (`SharesAsMoney.oneperiod_price_level`); `β ≠ 1/β` for `0 < β < 1` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_beta_wrong`). With `1 + y` bonds as numeraire, `P_t = (1 + r)/(1 + y)` (`SharesAsMoney.numeraire_one_plus_y`). |
| §8.4, p. 250 (E8.3) | "If `B^{(t+τ)}E_t(1/P_{t+τ})` matches the path of expected real surpluses, real-rate changes cancel." | True under perfect foresight, in continuous time (`MaturityBuffer.matching_cancels`, `MaturityBuffer.price_insulated`), and conversely only matching insulates the price level, in discrete time (`MaturityBuffer.price_insulated_only_if_matching`). Under uncertainty, with rates correlated with the price level, expectations of products do not factor: in the coin-flip tree `E₀[D/P_τ] = 1` while `E₀[Ds_τ] = 3/4` (`MaturityBuffer.matching_fails_under_uncertainty`). |

## Claims that need a changed statement

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §9.2, p. 264 (E9.1) | `lim_T βᵀE_tV*_{t+T} = 0` "for bounded `{s_{0,t}}`". | A hypothesis is missing: `0 < α < 2`. With it the limit is zero (`InflationTarget.tendsto_state_forecast`); with `s₀ = 0` and `V*_t ≠ 0` it is zero iff `0 < α < 2` (`InflationTarget.tendsto_state_forecast_iff`); at `α = 0` it is `V*_t − ∑ βʲE_ts_{0,t+j}` (`InflationTarget.tendsto_state_forecast_alpha_zero`). Theorem IT carries the hypothesis (`InflationTarget.price_eq_target`). |
| §9.2, p. 264 (E9.2) | "From (9.1) we have `P_0 = P*_0`", and by induction `P_t = P*_t`. | (9.1) is `V*_1 = B_0/P*_1` and gives `P_1 = P*_1` in book dating. The induction step divides by `V*_t − s_t`, so it needs `V*_t ≠ s_t` (`InflationTarget.price_eq_target`). Under perfect foresight the condition is not needed (`InflationTarget.price_eq_target_perfectForesight`); under uncertainty it is: with the debt fully repaid at date 0, the coin-flip tree has a sunspot date-1 price level (`InflationTarget.zero_debt_sunspot`). |
| §8.5, p. 255 (E8.1) | A 10% haircut with surpluses from `t + 1` on 10% lower "raises the price level today by 10%". | The exact factor is `(s_t + V)/(s_t + 0.9V)` (`Default.haircut_ten_percent`). It is `10/9`, an 11.1% rise, iff `s_t = 0`, and below `10/9` when `s_t > 0` (`Default.haircut_factor_eq_iff`, `Default.haircut_factor_lt`). The value of money `1/P` falls by exactly 10% iff `s_t = 0` (`Default.money_value_ten_percent_iff`), so "10%" is first-order. |
| §8.3, p. 248 (E8.4) | $10 of pennies: a fall of 1 in the present value raises the price level by 10%. | Exactly `10/9`, 11.1%; "10%" is the first-order elasticity (`CapitalStructure.pennies_inflation`). |
| §9.3.5, p. 271 | "If nominal debt is only 10% of all debt, then a 10% change in the price level only affects the value of government bonds by 1%." | The 1% is the elasticity (`MixedDebtRules.elasticity_value`). Exactly, a 10% rise lowers the debt value by `1/110` and a 10% fall raises it by `1/90` (`MixedDebtRules.ten_percent_rise`, `MixedDebtRules.ten_percent_fall`). |
| (8.1), p. 239 (E8.2) | "Iterating forward" gives `b_{t−1} = ∑ βʲs_{t+j}`. | The transversality condition `βᵀb_{t+T} → 0` must be explicit. With it, (8.1) holds pathwise as a convergent series, with no summability and no sign of `β` (`PresentValue.real_flow_tvc_iff`, `RealDebt.indexed_valuation_iff`); a `tsum` needs summability as well (`RealDebt.indexed_valuation_tsum`). |
| §9.3.4, p. 270 (E9.3) | The valuation (9.16) converges for `θ_{iπ} < 1`. | Convergence needs `\|βθ_{iπ}\| < 1` and `\|βη\| < 1`, hypotheses of `FiscalTaylor.eq_9_16` (via `FiscalTaylor.summable_coefSeq`, `FiscalTaylor.tsum_coefSeq`). The typo in the same paragraph is listed below. |
| (9.23) footnote, p. 276 (E9.5) | `r_t = (π^{e*} − E_tπ_{t+1})/(1 − θ)`, used for every `θ`. | The formula is undefined at the spread target `θ = 1` (`SpreadTarget.footnote_r`). The real rate is pinned down for every `θ` by `σκr_t = E_tπ_{t+1} − π_t` (`SpreadTarget.r_eq`), and the `θ = 1` case is proved directly (`SpreadTarget.theta_one`); it equals the `θ → 1` limit (`SpreadTarget.tendsto_coef_theta_one`). |
| p. 278 (E9.7) | "The IS shock does not appear in equilibrium output." | True given inflation: `κx_t = π_t − βπ^{e*} − u_{π,t}` (`SpreadTarget.x_formula`). Unexpected inflation loads on the real rate, which contains `u_x` (`SpreadTarget.r_formula`), so `u_x` can enter output through `ΔE_tπ_t`; this second step is not separately formalised. |
| p. 296 (E10.4) | Add "`M_t < min(s_{t+1})`" to rule out default. | A weak inequality suffices: with `M_t ≤ s_{t+1}` in every state there is no default and the bonds are worth exactly `βM_t` (`SharesAsMoney.bond_value_no_default`). |
| §8.3, p. 246 | More real and less nominal debt makes inflation more volatile. | Stated exactly: the elasticity of the price level to the present value is `−V/(V − b)` (`CapitalStructure.log_price_elasticity`), above one in size iff `b > 0` and strictly increasing in `b` (`CapitalStructure.elasticity_gt_one_iff`, `CapitalStructure.elasticity_strictMono`), and for any two present values the log price spread rises with `b` (`CapitalStructure.log_price_spread_strictMono`). |
| §8.3, p. 248 | The cashless limit is "determinate all the way along" but not at the limit. | Stated as: along `b_n → V` with `B_n = P(V − b_n)`, `P` is the unique solution at every `n` and nominal debt vanishes (`CapitalStructure.cashless_sequence_determinate`, `CapitalStructure.cashless_nominal_vanishes`), the elasticity diverges (`CapitalStructure.cashless_elasticity_explodes`), and at the limit every positive `P` solves (8.3) (`CapitalStructure.cashless_limit_indeterminate`). |
| §10.1.1, p. 289 | "It is enough that their value does not decline one-for-one with the price level." | Stated as: if the nominal value `Pb(P)` is strictly increasing, `B/P = b(P)` has at most one positive solution (`PotOfAssets.pot_unique_of_strictMono`); existence needs continuity and `B` between the end values (`PotOfAssets.pot_exists_of_continuous`). |
| §9.3.1, p. 265; §9.3.5, p. 271 | Surpluses that respond to the price level determine it. | Uniqueness for a strictly increasing rule (`PresentValue.price_unique_of_strictMonoOn`), existence and uniqueness when the debt is in its range (`FiscalRules.existsUnique_price`). With nominal debt a nondecreasing, even constant, surplus suffices (`MixedDebtRules.price_unique`, `MixedDebtRules.price_unique_const`), and multiplicity requires a falling surplus (`MixedDebtRules.multiplicity_needs_decreasing`). |
| §8.4, pp. 250–251 | "Otherwise, the mismatch … determines how the price level reacts to real interest rates." | The converse of matching is proved in discrete time, with real rates bounded below by some `r̲ > 0` and bounded payoffs (`MaturityBuffer.matching_necessary`, `MaturityBuffer.price_insulated_only_if_matching`); the continuous-time converse is not formalised. |
| §9.3.2, p. 266 | The valuation equation and the surplus rule determine `∑ βʲP_{t+j}` "but not the shape of that path". | Stated as: any bounded deviation with zero discounted sum gives another equilibrium (`FiscalRules.hyperplane_equilibrium`), with an explicit pair under perfect foresight (`FiscalRules.not_determined_by_valuation`). |
