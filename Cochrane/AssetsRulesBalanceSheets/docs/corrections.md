# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapters 8–10 of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of
the Price Level*, or in the two later claims formalised here (§11.5, p. 322, and §24.3, p. 527),
is false, imprecise, or relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs
from the printed one. In each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the
original.

Codes E8.1–E8.4, E9.1–E9.10 and E10.1–E10.4 are the Chapter 8–10 items of the survey of Chapters
6–10 (`notes/survey_cochrane_ch6-10.md`). Each was confirmed or refuted by formalisation before
being recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be
wrong. E9.9 was the survey's open question for these chapters; it is now settled (first
section). E8.5, an index slip in (8.4), is not in the survey and was found during formalisation.
E10.3 turned out not to be an error, and E9.6 and E9.10 were checked correct; they are listed in
the last section with the other claims that the book asserts without proof and that turned out
to be correct. Four items of this project's own earlier record of Theorem FR (d) are corrected in
a separate section; they are not book errors.

Out of scope are the institutional and historical passages: §8.2's interpretation of pegs and the
gold standard, the corporate-finance and moral-hazard discussion of §§8.3–8.4, most of §8.6, §9.1,
the verbal parts of §9.6, and §§10.2–10.7 beyond the balance-sheet identities. Lean names are
relative to the namespace `Cochrane.AssetsRulesBalanceSheets`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| §9.3.2, p. 267 (E9.9) | With the dynamic fiscal rule and indexed debt, and the interest-rate target (9.12), "only the sequence `P_t = P*_t` satisfies both (9.9) and (9.12)". | **False under uncertainty in general.** An explicit infinite settle-or-continue tree is an equilibrium for every `0 < β < 1`, every `0 < P_0 < 1 = P*_0`, every `θ` and every initial debt `b̄` equal to the present value of `s₀`: it has genuine probabilities in `(0, 1)`, satisfies (9.12) at every node, (9.8) on every edge with predetermined real debt, (9.9) at every node as a genuine convergent series of conditional expectations and ex post along every settling path, and the transversality condition (`FiscalRules.Sunspot.sunspot_counterexample`, `FiscalRules.Sunspot.valuation_expost`). **True under perfect foresight** (`FiscalRules.fr_perfectForesight`, `FiscalRules.fr_exists`). In general only `P_0 ≤ P*_0` survives, by Jensen's inequality (`FiscalRules.fr_jensen`). **Settled:** under any expectation system with the pull-out property, with `P* ≡ 1`, predetermined real debt, initial debt equal to the present value of `s₀` and the transversality condition, a price level that is **bounded above, or bounded away from zero**, satisfies (9.9) and (9.12) only if `P ≡ P*` (`FiscalRules.fr_unique_bounded`, via `FiscalRules.unique_of_ratio_tvc`). The counterexample's prices are unbounded above and tend to zero (`FiscalRules.Sunspot.settlePrice_unbounded`, `FiscalRules.Sunspot.contPrice_tendsto_zero`), so it violates both bounds. Corrected statement: add "among price paths bounded above, or bounded away from zero". **On a genuine probability space:** the same tree is a measure-theoretic expectation system on the settlement date, with infinitely many atoms and Mathlib's `condExp` equal to the tree's conditional expectations at every state; there the sunspot path and `P = P*` are both equilibria with initial debt `b̄`, and the sunspot prices are unbounded on both sides (`FiscalRulesMeasure.SunspotMeasure.fr_b_measure`, `FiscalRulesMeasure.SunspotMeasure.sun_E_node`, `FiscalRulesMeasure.SunspotMeasure.sun_price_unbounded`). Theorem FR (d) for a stochastic target and any initial debt is in the next section. |
| Bullet, p. 280 (E9.8) | "Offering to freely exchange real debt for nominal debt at the rate `P*`" drives the expected price level to `E_t(1/P_{t+1}) = 1/P*`, and the opportunity disappears. | It does not, with surpluses and the initial sales held fixed. The book's own differential `de/dB = −(e − 1/P*)/B` (`SpreadSwap.hasDerivAt_eSwap`) integrates to the hyperbola `e(B) − 1/P* = C/B` with `C = B₀(e₀ − 1/P*)` (`SpreadSwap.eSwap_sub_target`, `SpreadSwap.gap_const`). The target is reached at a finite `B` iff the gap was zero to begin with (`SpreadSwap.swap_reaches_target_iff`). When the public's real debt is exhausted the gap is still `C/(B₀ + P*b₀)` (`SpreadSwap.real_debt_exhausted`); in the survey's example it falls only from `7/30` to `35/222` (`SpreadSwap.numeric_example`). The direction is right (`SpreadSwap.eSwap_strictAntiOn`, `SpreadSwap.eSwap_gt_target`). Corrected statement: swaps at `P*` leave `b + B/P*` unchanged, and the target holds iff `β(b + B/P*) = W`, which a passive supply of nominal debt sold for goods reaches at `B = P*(W/β − b)` (`SpreadSwap.swap_invariant`, `SpreadSwap.passive_supply`). |
| (9.26), p. 277 (E9.4) | `σr_t = +ΔE_tπ_t/κ + u_{x,t} + u_{π,t} − E_tu_{π,t+1}`. | The correct formula is `σr_t = −ΔE_tπ_t/κ + u_{x,t} + (u_{π,t} − E_tu_{π,t+1})/κ` (`SpreadTarget.r_formula`); the difference is `SpreadTarget.printed_minus_corrected`. The missing `1/κ` is refuted under perfect foresight (`SpreadTarget.printed_9_26_fails_perfectForesight`) and the sign in the coin-flip tree (`SpreadTarget.printed_9_26_fails_coin`). |
| p. 296 (E10.1) | With the perpetuity as numeraire, `P_t = (1 + r)y/((1 + y)r)`. | (10.7) gives `P_t = (1 + y)r/((1 + r)y)` (`SharesAsMoney.perpetuity_price_level`, `SharesAsMoney.perpetuity_price_level_process`); the printed formula is its reciprocal (`SharesAsMoney.printed_reciprocal`), and the two agree iff `y = r` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_eq_iff`): at `r = .04`, `y = .05` they are `21/26` and `26/21` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_counterexample`). The book's conclusion "define `y = r` so `P_t = 1`" stands (`SharesAsMoney.price_level_one_iff`). |
| p. 296 (E10.2) | With one-period indexed bonds as numeraire, "`P_t = β`", and each maturing bond is redeemed for `β` new bonds. | A bond is worth `β` goods (`SharesAsMoney.oneperiod_real_value`), so the price level is `P_t = 1/β` and each maturing bond is redeemed for `1/β` new bonds (`SharesAsMoney.oneperiod_price_level`); `β ≠ 1/β` for `0 < β < 1` (`SharesAsMoney.printed_beta_wrong`). With `1 + y` bonds as numeraire, `P_t = (1 + r)/(1 + y)` (`SharesAsMoney.numeraire_one_plus_y`). |
| §8.4, p. 250 (E8.3) | "If `B^{(t+τ)}E_t(1/P_{t+τ})` matches the path of expected real surpluses, real-rate changes cancel." | True under perfect foresight, in continuous time (`MaturityBuffer.matching_cancels`, `MaturityBuffer.price_insulated`), and conversely only matching insulates the price level, in discrete time (`MaturityBuffer.price_insulated_only_if_matching`) and in continuous time (`TidyR5Matching.price_insulated_only_if_matching_cont`, `TidyR5MatchingMeas.price_insulated_only_if_matching_meas`). Under uncertainty, with rates correlated with the price level, expectations of products do not factor: in the coin-flip tree `E₀[D/P_τ] = 1` while `E₀[Ds_τ] = 3/4` (`MaturityBuffer.matching_fails_under_uncertainty`). |
