# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapter 7 of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of the
Price Level*, or in the Online Appendix sections it uses (§A1.2.1 and §A1.14), is false,
imprecise, or relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs from the
printed one. In each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the original.

Codes E7.1–E7.11 are the Chapter 7 items of the survey of Chapters 6–10
(`notes/survey_cochrane_ch6-10.md`). Each was confirmed or refuted by formalisation before being
recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be wrong.
E7.4 was the survey's open question; it is now settled (first section). Two findings, on strong
QE (p. 233) and on the reverse engineering of QE purchases (p. 230), are not in the survey and
were found during formalisation. Four precisions concern the book's deterministic passages taken
to uncertainty: §7.1 with a state-contingent rate path, §7.6.1 in both directions, §A1.14
with random debt, and the continuous-time forward-guidance formula of §A1.2.1 with a future rate
revealed as news (an extension: the book derives it for a known path). Several claims that the book asserts without proof turned out to be correct;
they are listed in the section "Claims confirmed correct". The last section records corrections
to our own earlier formalisation and README, which are not errors in the book.

Out of scope are §7.7 (the U.S. maturity structure, pp. 234–236), the narrative of runs and
defaults in §7.2.2 (pp. 219–220), and the discussion of precommitment and of the empirical record
of QE in §§7.6.2–7.6.3 (pp. 232–234). Lean names are relative to the namespace
`Cochrane.LongTermDebtDynamics`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (7.16)–(7.17), p. 225 (E7.4) | The present-value relations are linear equations `Bp = Rs`, "and hence" their solution is `p = B⁻¹Rs`; (7.17) is a formula for it. | The system need not determine the price level, and (7.17) can diverge. With `B_{t−1}^{(t)} = 1`, `B_{t−1}^{(t+1)} = 2/β`, a constant surplus `s > 0` and `0 < β < 1`, every path `1/P_t = x* + ε(−1/2)^t`, `x* = s/(3(1 − β))`, `\|ε\| < x*`, is positive and bounded and satisfies every flow and every present-value relation (`GeneralFormula.cx_flow`, `GeneralFormula.cx_presentValue`, `GeneralFormula.cx_pos`, `GeneralFormula.cx_bounded`, `GeneralFormula.cx_ne`). Its weights are `W^{(k)} = (β + 2(−2/β)^k)/(β + 2)` and (7.17) diverges (`GeneralFormula.cx_weight`, `GeneralFormula.cx_formula_diverges`). **Settled:** the price level is determined (a) when the government never buys back debt, with nonnegative inherited debt and positive debt coming due: two positive paths satisfying every flow and present-value relation coincide, under uncertainty (`GeneralFormula.price_unique_noBuyback`); or (b) under the row condition `\|A_t^{(t+j+1)}\| ≤ a_j`, `∑ β^{j+1}a_j ≤ ρ < 1`, among paths with bounded real maturing debt, and then (7.17) converges absolutely and is the price level (`GeneralFormula.price_unique_row`, `GeneralFormula.theoremW`, `GeneralFormula.price_formula`). In general, given one bounded solution, uniqueness holds iff the homogeneous system has no nonzero bounded solution (`GeneralFormula.unique_iff_homogeneous`). The counterexample buys back debt and violates the row condition (`GeneralFormula.cx_buyback`, `GeneralFormula.cx_not_row`). |
| (7.15), p. 225 (E7.2) | `∑_j E_t(1/P_{t+j})B_{t−1}^{(t+j)} = E_t∑_j β^js_{t+j}`. | The left side omits `β^j`; the relation is (7.5), `MaturityStructure.PresentValue`. With one unit of debt due at each of dates 0 and 1, surpluses `s_0 = s_1 = 1` and `1/P_t = 1`, every flow condition and the corrected relation hold, but the printed one would require `2 = 1 + β` (`GeneralFormula.printed_715_fails`). |
| (7.16), p. 225 (E7.3) | The matrix display lists row `t` as `B_t^{(t+1)}, B_t^{(t+2)}, …`, without discount factors, and row 1 as `B_1^{(2)}, B_1^{(3)}, B_1^{(3)}, …`. | Row `t` must start with `B_{t−1}^{(t)}` in column `t` (the printed row is one index too late), its entries carry `β^{m−t}`, and the repeated `B_1^{(3)}` should be `B_1^{(4)}`. The corrected matrix is `GeneralFormula.pvMatrix`; applied to `(1/P_m)` its row `t` is the real value of debt (`GeneralFormula.pvMatrix_row`). |
| p. 233 (found during formalisation) | Under strong QE, `B_0^{(j)} = P_js_j`, a 1% cut in bond supply gives a 1% lower future price level and "a one percentage point decrease in bond price". | The sign of the bond-price effect is wrong. With `P_0` fixed and `Q_0^{(j)} = β^jP_0/P_j`, scaling supply by `λ` scales `P_j` by `λ` and `Q_0^{(j)}` by `1/λ`, so a 1% cut (`λ = 0.99`) raises the bond price by between 1.01% and 1.02% (`QuantitativeEasing.strong_qe`). The price-level half of the sentence is correct. |

## Claims that need a changed statement

| Where | Book says | Finding |

| §7.1, p. 214 (E7.6) | "If `T > k`, and forward guidance exceeds the longest outstanding maturity", `P_0` "does not decline at all". | `T ≥ k` suffices (`ForwardGuidance.priceLevel_fg_of_le`), and it is sharp: if a maturity `j` with `T < j ≤ k` is outstanding and all positions are nonnegative, guidance lowers `P_0` (`ForwardGuidance.priceLevel_fg_lt`). |
| §7.5, pp. 226–228 (E7.7) | "There is a debt policy … that achieves any price level path consistent with" (7.18), namely (7.19), `B_0^{(j)}E_0(1/P_j) = E_0s_j`. | Theorem P is proved with the hypotheses the policy needs: positive surpluses with positive date-0 expectations, positive targets `E_0(1/P_j)` known at date 0, consistency with (7.18) (`PolicyConstraints.Targets`), and quotients of known variables being known (`PolicyConstraints.DivClosed`, true for perfect foresight and every finite tree). Then (7.19) achieves the targets with positive price levels and satisfies every flow and present-value relation (`PolicyConstraints.polOne_achieves`, `PolicyConstraints.polOne_pos`, `PolicyConstraints.polOne_flow`, `PolicyConstraints.polOne_presentValue`); (7.20) does too (`PolicyConstraints.polTwo_achieves`). The converse, that every equilibrium satisfies (7.18), needs none of them (`PolicyConstraints.presentValue_zero_nominal`). |
| §7.5, p. 228 (E7.8) | "A QE policy that raises near-term price levels with no decline in future price levels is not possible." | True with nonnegative positions `B_{−1}^{(j)} ≥ 0` (`PolicyConstraints.qe_tradeoff`, `PolicyConstraints.qe_tradeoff_strict`), false without: with `B_{−1}^{(0)} = 1` and `B_{−1}^{(1)} = −1`, raising both `P_0` and `P_1` preserves (7.18) (`PolicyConstraints.qe_negative_position`). |
| §7.6.1, p. 230 (found during formalisation) | For a desired price-level path and a choice of one of `M` or `B̃`, (7.23) "gives us the other one". | True in the direction "given `B̃`, find `M`": the reserves exist, are unique and are explicit (`QuantitativeEasing.reserves_formula`, `QuantitativeEasing.reserves_unique`). In the direction "given `M`, find `B̃`" the purchases are unique when they exist (`QuantitativeEasing.qe_unique`), and they exist when the tail series of the residual `R_t` converge, for example when it is bounded (`QuantitativeEasing.qe_exists`, `QuantitativeEasing.qe_residual_summable`). Existence requires `β^NR_{t+N} → 0` (`QuantitativeEasing.qe_exists_necessary`) and fails, for any positive price path, for reserves that make `R_t = β^{−t}` (`QuantitativeEasing.qe_counterexample`). |
| (7.17), p. 225 | (7.17) holds for any debt policy. | It is an exact identity when all debt matures by a finite date (`GeneralFormula.theoremW_finite`). With an infinite horizon it is proved under the row condition (`GeneralFormula.theoremW`), also with random, date-dependent bounds `∑_j β^{j+1}\|A_t^{(t+j+1)}(ω)\| ≤ ρ < 1` and for every expectation system (`TidyR5SeriesExchange.theoremW_rowR'`, `TidyR5RandomRow.existsUnique_bounded_solution`), and without buy-backs, where it solves the flow system with no row condition (`TidyR5NoBuybackExistence.formulaW_solves_noBuyback`, `TidyR5NoBuybackExistence.maturing_eq_formulaW`); without a condition it is false (E7.4, above). Each sale fraction `A_t^{(t+j+1)}` is assumed known at `t + j`, the date of the last sale before maturity (`GeneralFormula.weight_known`). |
| §7.1, pp. 213–215, under uncertainty | The bond-price display, the price path and "no second jump", in a deterministic analysis. | With random surpluses and a rate path known at date 0 the display holds in every state (`TidyR5GuidanceUncertainty.stoch_fgPrice`), but the book's price path is the path of `E_0(1/P_j)` (`TidyR5GuidanceUncertainty.stoch_E0_path`), and "no second jump" becomes `E_t(P_t/P_{t+1}) = 1` for `t < T`: the realised price level moves with news only (`TidyR5GuidanceUncertainty.stoch_no_expected_jump`). A precision, not an error. |
| §7.1, pp. 213–214, state-contingent guidance | An announced rate rise lowers `P_0`; the effect falls with the horizon; guidance has less effect than the same immediate rise. | True for any rate path known at date 0, state by state (`TidyR5GuidanceUncertainty.stoch_fg_lowers`, `TidyR5GuidanceUncertainty.stoch_fg_mono`). All three fail for a state-contingent path. Sign: with `r = 1`, a date-1 rate of `2` or `1/5` (mean `11/10 > r`), `r` at every other date, `B_{−1}^{(2)} = 1` and `s ≡ 1`, `P_0 = 7/48` against `1/8` with no announcement, since `Q_0^{(2)} = 7/24 > 1/4` (Jensen, `E_0(1+i_1)^{−1} > (1+E_0i_1)^{−1}`) (`TidyR5RandomGuidance.cg_counterexample`). Horizon and immediate rise: the same shape delayed one period gives `P_0 = 1/8`, so the earlier announcement has the higher price level (`TidyR5RandomHorizon.cg_horizon_counterexample`); the covariance term of `TidyR5RandomGuidance.bondPrice_cov_rate` is zero there (`TidyR5RandomHorizon.cg_cov_zero`). What survives: bonds maturing by `T` are unaffected and guidance with `T ≥ k` has no effect (`TidyR5RandomGuidance.random_fg_no_effect`). Equilibrium there means, as in the book's §7.1, the Fisher relations plus the date-0 valuation (7.3); both counterexamples survive with the flow conditions (7.1) and present-value relations (7.2) at every date under the rollover policy `TidyR6GuidanceFlows.rollD`, which pins `P_0` uniquely, `7/48` against `1/8` (`TidyR6GuidanceFlows.cg_flows_counterexample`, `TidyR6GuidanceFlows.cg_horizon_flows_counterexample`). A precision: the book's §7.1 is deterministic. |
| App. p. 561, under uncertainty (extension) | The continuous-time forward-guidance formula `P_0/P − 1 = e^{−(r+ϖ)T}((r+ϖ)/(i+ϖ) − 1)`, derived for a known rate path. | Not an error in the book: for a known announced path it holds verbatim, state by state, with Brownian noise in the nominal discount factor and random surpluses (`TidyR7GuidanceItoValuation.guidance_jump_ito`; see "Claims confirmed correct"). If the rate `ι` from `T` on is news revealed at `T`, reading the formula at the expected rate is false. The correct statement is `P_0/P_0' − 1 = E_0[e^{rT}Λ_T · cfgJump r ι ϖ T]/Λ_0`, the book's jump at each realised rate averaged under the `T`-forward measure (`TidyR7GuidanceItoValuation.random_jump_ito`). On the constructed Brownian motion with `ι = i₁` if `W_T > 0` and `i₂` otherwise, `J(x) = cfgJump r x ϖ T`: without inflation risk the jump `E[J(ι)]` strictly exceeds the formula at the expected rate, `J(E ι)`, because `J` is strictly convex in the rate (`TidyR7GuidanceItoCex.bm_jensen`, `TidyR7GuidanceItoCex.cfgJump_strictConvex`); with inflation risk `κ ≠ 0` the jump differs from the average `E[J(ι)]` by the covariance `κm(J(i₁) − J(i₂)) ≠ 0` of the forward weight with `J(ι)` (`TidyR7GuidanceItoCex.bm_covariance`, `TidyR7GuidanceItoCex.bm_cov_identity`); and for some `κ ≠ 0`, `J(E ι) < E[J(ι)] < P_0/P_0' − 1` (`TidyR7GuidanceItoCex.bm_random_headline`). The same holds for a continuous-valued random rate `i` after `T` (any bounded jointly measurable rate): `P_0/P_0' − 1 = E_0[e^{rT}Λ_T · rateJump]/Λ_0`, the pathwise jump at the realised rate path averaged under the `T`-forward measure (`TidyR9GuidanceRandomRate.random_rate_jump`), the mean jump plus a covariance term (`TidyR9GuidanceRandomRate.random_rate_jump_decomp`); on the constructed Brownian motion with `i_u = ī + c sin W_u`, `c ≠ 0`, so that `E i_u = ī`, and `Λ > 0` throughout, the formula at the expected rate is strictly below the jump, `cfgJump r ī ϖ T < P_0/P_0' − 1`, by Jensen alone (`TidyR9GuidanceRandomRateBM.bm_sinRate_jump`). |
| §7.6.1, p. 230, under uncertainty | For a desired price-level path and one of `M` or `B̃`, (7.23) "gives us the other one". | Holds under perfect foresight, the book's own assumption ("I drop `E_0`", p. 230) (`TidyR5QEUncertainty.perfectForesight_reserves_exist`). Under uncertainty, predetermined reserves exist iff the implied reserves `R_t` are known at `t − 1` (`TidyR5QEUncertainty.reserves_exist_iff`), and QE purchases fixed at date 0 exist iff every implied purchase `c_t = (R_t − βE_tR_{t+1})P_t` is known at date 0 (`TidyR5QEReverse.qe_exists_iff`). With a coin-flip surplus shock at date 1 and a deterministic desired path neither exists (`TidyR5QEUncertainty.coin_no_reserves`, `TidyR5QEReverse.coin_no_qe`). A precision, not an error. |
| App. p. 592, with random debt | The sign of the effect of expected future sales on `P_0`, derived with deterministic debt. | It holds for state-contingent sales `G ≥ 0` with random debt and surpluses, using `s_2 ≥ 0` (`TidyR5FutureSalesRandom.x0R_mono`, `TidyR5FutureSalesRandom.x0R_anti`, `TidyR5FutureSalesRandom.x0R_const`), which is no extra restriction since positive `P_2` forces `s_2 > 0` (`TidyR5FutureSalesRandom.price_two_pos_iff`). The strict version holds under the book's sign conditions alone exactly where the larger sale has positive conditional probability, `E_0 1_{G>0} > 0` (`TidyR6StrictSignRule.x0R_strict_iff`), in every state when `G > 0` everywhere (`TidyR6StrictSignRule.x0R_strictMono_pos`), and almost surely on a probability space iff `μ[1_{G>0} \| m] > 0`, also for equilibrium price levels (`TidyR6StrictSignMeasure.x0M_strict_iff_ae`, `TidyR6StrictSignMeasure.eqm_strict_iff_ae`). A precision. |
| §7.3.2, p. 224 | A state-contingent sale can offset a surplus shock. | Proved at date 0 with the revenue headroom stated: if the revenue from a maturity with outstanding debt can still rise by the size of the shock, a larger sale raises exactly that revenue and leaves `P_0` unchanged, raising the future price level (`BondSales.offset_shock`). |
