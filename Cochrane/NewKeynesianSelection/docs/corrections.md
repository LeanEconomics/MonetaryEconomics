# Corrections to the source

This file lists places where a claim in Chapter 16 (with the precise claims of Chapter 12 and
Online Appendix A3) of Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level*, is false,
imprecise, or relies on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs from the
printed one. In each case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the original.

Codes E1–E6 are the Chapter 16 items of the survey of Chapters 11–19
(`notes/survey_cochrane_ch11-19.md`). Each was confirmed or refuted by formalisation before being
recorded here, and an entry is revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be wrong: the
survey's correction of E6 was wrong, and is replaced below. Several claims that the book asserts
without proof turned out to be correct; they are listed in the last section.

Out of scope are the verbal and empirical passages: central-bank communication and history, the
Smets–Wouters and Clarida–Galí–Gertler estimates (p. 381), and the remark that removing the bound
would allow global instability (p. 380, covered only through the Bassetto results). Lean names are
relative to the namespace `Cochrane.NewKeynesianSelection`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p. 396 (E1) | Sims' tiny fiscal model, `s_t = γb_t + θπ_t`, `b_{t+1} = Rb_t − s_t`: for `φ > 1`, "for any `π₀ ≠ 0`, debt explodes, violating the transversality condition", so the nominal explosions are ruled out. | Debt does explode for `\|R − γ\| < 1 < φ` and `θπ₀ ≠ 0` (`SimsTinyFiscal.debt_explodes`), but the transversality condition `R^{−t}b_t → 0` holds iff `θπ₀ = 0` or `φ < R` (`SimsTinyFiscal.tvc_iff`). So it fails for every `π₀ ≠ 0` **iff `φ ≥ R`** (`SimsTinyFiscal.e1_tvc_violated_iff`), and the transversality condition selects `π₀ = 0` exactly then (`SimsTinyFiscal.active_iff`). For `1 < φ < R` fiscal policy stays passive and the multiplicity remains. Counterexample: `R = 1.05`, `γ = 0.5`, `φ = 1.02`, `θ = π₀ = 1`, `b₀ = 0`: debt explodes, yet `R^{−t}b_t → 0` (`SimsTinyFiscal.e1_counterexample`). |
| p. 395 (E1, second part) | "Specialize to `γ > R − 1` so debt is stationary." | Debt `(R − γ)ᵗb₀` converges for every `b₀` iff `R − 1 < γ < R + 1` (`SimsTinyFiscal.stationary_iff`). With `R = 1.05`, `γ = 3.05 > R − 1` gives `b_t = (−2)ᵗ` (`SimsTinyFiscal.stationary_counterexample`). |
| p. 379 (E2) | "the bound `i ≥ 0` means we cannot have `Π_{t+1} ≤ β`", so `Φ` "must also have another stationary point, labeled `Π_L`. This stationary point must be stable, with `Φ′(Π_L) < 1`." | Three corrections. (i) The bound is weak: `i_t ≥ 0` iff `Π_{t+1} ≥ β`, and `Π_{t+1} = β` is the zero rate (`Hyperinflation.zero_bound_iff_scalar`, `Hyperinflation.zero_bound_iff`, `LowerBound.zero_bound_eq_iff`). (ii) `Π_L` exists if `Φ` is continuous on `[β, Π*]` with `Φ ≥ β` there; then the largest fixed point below `Π*` has `Φ < id` on `(Π_L, Π*)` and `Φ′(Π_L) ≤ 1` (`LowerBound.lower_steady_state`). Without continuity there need be no `Π_L`: `phiJump` respects the bound, has `Π* = 2` with `Φ′(Π*) = 2`, and no fixed point in `[β, Π*)` (`LowerBound.e2_discontinuous_counterexample`). (iii) Only `Φ′(Π_L) ≤ 1` follows. With `β = 1/2`, `Φ(Π) = max(1/2, Π + (Π − 1/2)(Π − 1)²(Π − 2))` has `Π* = 2`, `Φ′(Π*) = 5/2`, and `Π_L = 1` with `Φ′(Π_L) = 1` (`LowerBound.e2_tangency_counterexample`). There `Π_L` is not stable from the left (`LowerBound.phiTan_left_unstable`), but the economic claim survives: paths from `(Π_L, Π*)` converge to `Π_L` (`LowerBound.phiTan_deflation_tendsto`). |
| p. 365 (E4) | After (16.8): `p_t = p* − ∑ (1 + φ)^{−(j+1)}E_tu_{t+j}`, the unique locally bounded price level "if `φ_p > 0`". | The coefficient is `φ_p`, not `φ`: `p_t = p* − ∑ (1 + φ_p)^{−(j+1)}E_tu_{t+j}` (`SimplestModel.wicksell_apply`). Under perfect foresight with `φ_p = 1`, `φ = 2`, `u ≡ 1`, the printed gap `−1/2` does not solve (16.8) and the corrected gap `−1` does (`SimplestModel.printed_wicksell_false`). The price gap is determinate iff `\|1 + φ_p\| > 1`, i.e. `φ_p > 0` **or `φ_p < −2`** (`SimplestModel.wicksell_determinate_iff`). |
| p. 373 (E6) | The active-money passive-fiscal regime of the unified model (16.20)–(16.22) is "`φ > 1`, `γ > 0`, most simply `γ = α > 0`". | With (16.21)–(16.22) as printed, the actual surplus `s_{t+1}` enters both debt equations, so the debt gap `e = v − v*` obeys `ρe_{t+1} = e_t − (ΔE_{t+1}π_{t+1} − ΔE_{t+1}π*_{t+1})` and `γ` drops out (`ObservationalEquivalence.e_dyn`). For `0 < ρ < 1`, `0 < α < 2` and a bounded surplus disturbance, `ρ^TE_tv_{t+T} → ((α − γ)/α)(v_t − v*_t)` (`ObservationalEquivalence.tendsto_tvc`). Fiscal policy is therefore passive **iff `γ = α`** (`ObservationalEquivalence.passive_iff`), not for every `γ > 0`. Every `γ ≠ α` makes the fiscal rule active as well, so both policies select `π = π*` (`ObservationalEquivalence.not_passive_of_ne`, `ObservationalEquivalence.am_regime`). Counterexample on the coin-flip tree: `ρ = α = 1/2`, `γ = 1` is not passive (`ObservationalEquivalence.e6_printed_fails`, `ObservationalEquivalence.coin_passive_iff`, `ObservationalEquivalence.e6_counterexample`). |
| E6 as corrected by the survey | Passivity "needs `0 < γ < 2`". | Also wrong for (16.20)–(16.22) as printed: `γ = 1` lies in `(0, 2)` and is not passive (`ObservationalEquivalence.e6_printed_fails`). The range `0 < γ < 2` is the passivity range of a rule on actual debt, `s_{t+1} = γv_t + u_{s,t+1}` of (5.36)–(5.37), which is (16.20) with `γ = α`; for the §16.8 rule `s_t = γB_{t−1}/P_t` it is proved as `LowerBound.passive_gamma_tvc_iff`. |

## Claims that need a changed statement

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p. 364 | If `\|φ\| < 1`, "expected inflation `E_tπ_{t+j}` converges going forward for any initial value." | True for a constant disturbance, with limit `c/(1 − φ)` (`SimplestModel.expected_const_converges`), and for the sunspot component (`SimplestModel.sunspot_fades`). For a general bounded disturbance only boundedness holds (`SimplestModel.expected_bounded`). Counterexample: perfect foresight, `φ = 0`, `u_t = (−1)ᵗ`; `E_0π_j` oscillates between `1` and `−1` (`SimplestModel.converges_needs_convergent_u`). |
| (16.14), p. 367 | "If the central bank follows `φ > 1`, the only nonexplosive equilibrium is `π_t = π*_t`." | Proved for `\|φ\| > 1`, for any bounded adapted target and so for any bounded disturbance process (`KingForm.eq_target_of_bounded`, `KingForm.translation_bijOn`). |
| p. 373 (active-fiscal regime) | Active fiscal, passive money (most simply `φ = 0`) gives `π = π*`. | (16.22) starts at `t = 0`, so the transversality condition pins `ΔEπ = ΔEπ*` but leaves `π_t − π*_t = φᵗ(π_0 − π*_0)`, for every `φ`; `π = π*` needs `π_0 = π*_0`, a date-0 valuation condition outside (16.22) (`ObservationalEquivalence.af_regime`). With `φ = 0`, `π_t = π*_t` from date 1 on with no initial condition (`ObservationalEquivalence.af_regime_phi_zero`). |
| p. 375 (E5) | Open mouth: "`π_2 = π_2 = ..`" and "`u_1 = −φπ*_1 = −φ1`". | Typographical: `π_2 = π_3 = … = 0` and `u_1 = −φ·1`. With `η = 0` and `ε_1 = −φ`, `π_1 = 1`, `π_t = 0` for `t ≠ 1`, `i ≡ 0`, and the price level jumps permanently by one (`Responses.open_mouth`, `Responses.open_mouth_price_jump`, `Responses.open_mouth_coin`). |
| p. 376 | The rule `i_t = φE_tπ_{t+1}` "would lead to an equilibrium condition `φE_tπ_{t+1} = E_tπ_{t+1}` which has no solution". | Solutions exist: `π = 0`, `i = 0` for every `φ` (`Responses.ruleExp_has_solution`), and indeed every shock process with `i = 0` (`Responses.ruleExp_solutions`), so inflation is entirely indeterminate. The correct statement: for `φ ≠ 1` every equilibrium has `E_tπ_{t+1} = i_t = 0` (`Responses.ruleExp_trivial`). With a disturbance, `E_tπ_{t+1} = u_t/(1 − φ)` and the real rate is still zero (`Responses.ruleExp_disturbance`). |
| pp. 377–378 | Linearising (16.25) gives (16.1). | Proved exactly: under any `ExpectationSystem`, at any deterministic steady state `π*`, along every bounded direction `x` of log inflation, the derivative of `log(1 + i_t)` at `ε = 0` is `E_tx` (`LowerBound.gateaux_log_bondPricing`, from `LowerBound.hasDerivAt_log_bondPricing`). This is a Gateaux, not a Fréchet, derivative: `E_t` is an abstract linear map with no topology. |
| p. 382 | "`φ` is also identified if there is no disturbance … and if there are shocks to other equations." | In the simplest model, `θ = φ` with `u ≡ 0` does not identify `φ`: the only bounded target is `π* = 0`, so the data are identically zero (`Identification.theta_eq_phi_no_shock_zero`). Identification needs the shocks from other equations; with a real-rate shock, `φ = 2` is identified by IV (`Identification.rr_example`). |
| p. 394 | In the active-fiscal equilibrium, `θ < 1` "is identified, and hence measurable by econometricians and learnable to agents". | The restriction `θ < 1` is unnecessary: `θ` is identified and learnable for every `θ`, provided inflation is not identically zero (`Identification.ftpl_theta_identified`, `Identification.ftpl_theta_learnable`). |
| p. 385 (MSV) | "The minimum state variable criterion says to pick `π_t = f(u_t)`. The only such solution is … (16.6)." | True for linear `f`, under any `ExpectationSystem` (`SelectionPatches.msv_iff`). False for general `f` under perfect foresight: `f(x) = −x/(φ − η) + x^{log φ/log η}` gives a solution that differs from (16.6) by `φᵗu₀^κ`, explosive when `φ > 1` (`SelectionPatches.msv_nonlinear_counterexample`). The linear MSV solution is also selected for passive `\|φ\| < 1`, where bounded non-MSV solutions exist (`SelectionPatches.pf_passive_bounded_not_msv`), so MSV is a selection device independent of the Taylor principle. |
| (16.32), p. 393 | The difference equation (16.32) "may rise to require `Π_{t+1} = ∞` above some bound `Π_U`, even if the policy rule for nominal interest rates … remains bounded"; `L(y, Φ(Π))`. | Made exact. With a bounded continuous rule and `u_c ∘ L` continuous and positive on the range of rates the rule sets, every `Π_t > 0` has a finite successor and every path exists (`SelectionPatches.successor_exists_of_bounded_rule`, `SelectionPatches.path_exists_of_bounded_rule`). A finite-time explosion with a bounded rule therefore needs `u_c ∘ L` to vanish or blow up at a finite rate, and that does produce one: with `β = 1/2`, `Φ = max(1/2, min(2Π − 1, 3))`, `L(i) = 5 − i` and `u_c = 1/m`, no `Π_t ∈ (1, 3/2)` has a finite successor (`SelectionPatches.bounded_rule_explosion`). The Schmitt-Grohé–Uribe example uses an unbounded rule (`SelectionPatches.SGU.sgu_no_successor`). The second argument of `L` is the interest rate `Φ(Π)/β − 1`, not `Φ(Π)` (`SelectionPatches.eq1632_iff`). |
| p. 394 (Sims 1994) | A lower limit on nominal money demand makes real money explode in a deflation, violating the transversality condition. | True when the deflation approaches the zero-bound rate fast enough, `∑(Π_{t+1} − β) < ∞`, e.g. reaching `β` in finite time (`SelectionPatches.sims_tvc_violated`, `SelectionPatches.sims_tvc_violated_of_eventually_beta`). It fails when `Π_L > β` (`SelectionPatches.sims_no_bite_above_beta`) and for a slow convergence `Π_{t+1} = β(t + 2)/(t + 1)`, where `βᵀM̲/P_T = M̲/(T + 1) → 0` (`SelectionPatches.sims_slow_counterexample`). |
| §16.10.5, p. 392 | Rules touching the 45° line only at `Π*` (Alstadheim–Henderson) select `Π*`. | The working hypothesis is that the rule sends every `Π ≠ Π*` above `Π*` (`SelectionPatches.v_rule_unique_bounded`), as the V-shaped and the discontinuous rules do (`SelectionPatches.vRule_props`, `SelectionPatches.ahJump_props`). Touching the line only at `Π*` is not enough: the tangent rule `Φ(Π) = Π + (Π − Π*)²` has a continuum of bounded paths converging to `Π*` (`SelectionPatches.tangent_rule_converges`). |
| p. 379 (E3) | "Substituting the rule (2.22)" and `S_t` in (16.30). | A cross-reference slip: the rule substituted is the displayed `s_t = (r/(1+r))B_{t−1}/P_t` (`Hyperinflation.PassiveRule`), and `S_t` is `s_t`. (16.30) itself is correct (`Hyperinflation.real_debt_flow`). |

## A comparison with Woodford (not a book error)

Cochrane's passivity (the transversality condition along every inflation path) is strictly weaker
than Woodford's local Ricardianness (bounded debt). With `γ = α` the unified model's debt follows
Woodford's (4.5) with `τ_b = α` (`Links.fiscalBlock_debt45`, `Links.e6_passive_iff_woodford45`);
a locally Ricardian rule is passive (`Links.passive_of_ricardian`), but with `ρ = 1/2` and
`γ = α = 1/4` policy is passive while `|ρ⁻¹(1 − α)| = 3/2 > 1` (`Links.passive_not_ricardian`).
