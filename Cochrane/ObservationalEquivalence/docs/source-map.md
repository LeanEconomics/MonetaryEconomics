# Source map

Cochrane (2023), *The Fiscal Theory of the Price Level*, Chapter 22, "Observational equivalence"
(pp. 495–510), with the precise claims of Chapters 23–24 (pp. 512–523) and of Online Appendix
Chapter 2, "How not to test fiscal theory" (pp. 593–611). Each formal claim is listed with its
Lean name, relative to the namespace `Cochrane.ObservationalEquivalence`. Page numbers are book
pages. The book has no labelled propositions, so rows are keyed by equation number, section, the
survey's theorem names (OE-1, OE-2, NI-1 to NI-3, LR, RG) or page. Rows follow the book: the
valuation equation and the starred model of §22.1, observational equivalence, money regimes,
non-identification, the asset-pricing analogy of §22.2.4, the regressions and Granger causality of
§22.3, the Leeper regions of §22.7 and Chapter 23, Markov switching in Chapter 24, the
present-value identities of the Online Appendix, then the links between modules, the
measure-theoretic core and the shared tools. The genuine-probability versions of Theorem RG,
Granger causality and Markov switching follow the section they make genuine, and so do the
sections on the money model under uncertainty, Theorem RG and Granger causality with
square-integrable shocks, and the boundary `|φ| = 1` (after the Leeper regions). The household
problem behind the money model, its derived equilibria, the finite-tree and perfect-foresight
versions and the derivation of both halves of (22.2) follow the money model under uncertainty.

The linear results hold for an arbitrary `ExpectationSystem`, so under perfect foresight and
under uncertainty. The measure-theoretic modules (`ExpectationsMeasure` onward) restate Theorem RG,
the Granger news model and Davig–Leeper switching (with two regimes, with `N` regimes and
coefficients of any sign, and with only reachable regimes counting) on genuine probability spaces,
with Mathlib's `condExp`, `covariance` and `variance`; their equations hold almost everywhere.
`MarkovRegimesGeneral`, also in that range, is the `N`-regime regime tree. An *admissible* equilibrium has adapted, bounded inflation and debt satisfying
the transversality condition (`StarredModel.Admissible`); *unique* means the target is the only
admissible equilibrium (`Equivalence.UniqueEqm`). Survey error codes E12–E19 are those of the
survey of Chapters 20–25 (`notes/survey_cochrane_ch20-25.md`), and E6 that of the survey of
Chapters 11–19; see [corrections](corrections.md). Rows marked "restated" reproduce results
proved in an earlier project, with the namespace adapted.

## The household problem behind the money model, §22.1, pp. 495–496; (3.9), p. 57; §19.3, pp. 439–440; (19.24)–(19.28), pp. 448–449 (TidyR6MoneyHousehold)

The money modules above impose the Fisher equation, the quantity equation and the valuation
equation. `TidyR6MoneyHousehold` derives them from a risk-neutral household's problem (the
chapter's discounting `βʲ` of a random endowment), on any probability space with any filtration,
every constraint holding almost surely. Money held overnight costs `Q^m_t` (`Q^m = 1` for cash,
`Q^m = Q` for money paying the bond rate), and `ι_t = Q^m_t − Q_t` is the opportunity cost of
money.
