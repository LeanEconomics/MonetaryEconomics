# Corrections to the source

Places where a claim in Chapter 3 or Appendix B of Woodford (2003) is false, imprecise, or relies
on an unstated hypothesis, and where the Lean statement departs from the printed one. In each
case the Lean statement proves the stated version and cites the original. Entries are recorded
when found and revised if formalisation shows the finding itself to be wrong: in particular, the
p. 217 claim that output contracts on impact under indexation when nominal GDP rises with a lag
was at first left unproved; it holds for every `0 ≤ γ ≤ 1` (`Delays.p217_contraction_general`).
Table 3.1 is exact to two decimals, including two entries that sit on a rounding tie
(`StrategicComplementarity.table31_rounding`). One question is left open: for Prop 3.4 with
`ω̃θ < 0`, uniqueness outside the difference-stationary class is proved only under perfect
foresight (`Calvo.prop34_pf_unique_of_w_neg`); under genuine uncertainty the explosive
deterministic family is excluded (`Calvo.perturbation_not_summable`), but whether other solutions
exist outside the class is not decided, because deriving the recursive form from (2.8) without a
growth bound needs an interchange of `E_t` with an undominated series. Lean names are relative to
the namespace `Woodford.NominalRigidities`.

## Claims that are false as printed

| Where | Book says | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (1.14), p. 151 | (1.14) must have a unique solution `Yⁿ`. | Uniqueness holds; existence needs a boundary condition. Exactly: a natural rate exists iff `μ⁻¹` lies between two values of `s(Y, Y)` (`FlexiblePrices.naturalRate_existsUnique_iff`, `FlexiblePrices.isoelastic_naturalRate`). Counterexample: `v_h = μ⁻¹h/(1 + h)`, `u_c = 1 + 1/Y` and linear `f` meet every stated hypothesis and `s` is strictly increasing in both arguments, yet `s(Y, Y) < μ⁻¹` for all `Y`, so there is no flexible-price equilibrium (`FlexiblePrices.noNaturalRate_baseline`, `FlexiblePrices.baseline_noFlexEqm`). |
| Assumption 3.1, Prop 3.1, (1.19)–(1.21), p. 154 | Single-peakedness gives `Π₁` a unique zero, so (1.21) determines a unique natural rate. | Assumption 3.1 gives neither existence nor uniqueness. With `Π = P g(p/P)` and `g′ = (x − 1)²(2 − x)`, `Π₁` vanishes at `p = P` and `p = 2P`, so (1.21) holds for every `Y` while no flexible-price equilibrium exists (`FlexiblePrices.twoRoots_counterexample`); other examples have no natural rate or a continuum of them (`FlexiblePrices.noNaturalRate_counterexample`, `FlexiblePrices.continuumNaturalRate_counterexample`). Correct: an equilibrium is exactly a peak at `p = 1` (`FlexiblePrices.prop_3_1_characterisation`), equivalent to (1.21) when `Π₁` vanishes only at the peak (`FlexiblePrices.foc_iff_optimal`); `Yⁿ` exists and is unique when `Y ↦ Π₁(1, 1, 1; Y)` is continuous, strictly decreasing and changes sign (`FlexiblePrices.prop_3_1`). |
| (1.26), pp. 156–157 | `P` is implicitly defined by (1.26). | It need not exist or be unique. Under perfect foresight with `g(y) = (y − 1)(y − 2)` and `𝒴 = 2`, both `P = 1` and `P = 2` solve (1.26); with `g ≡ 1` nothing does (`Predetermined.priceLevel_not_unique_counterexample`, `Predetermined.priceLevel_not_exists_counterexample`). Correct: if each `g(ω, ·)` is continuous and strictly decreasing, positive at some `y_lo` and negative at some `y_hi`, there is a unique positive predetermined solution on every finite event tree, and it is homogeneous of degree 1 in spending (`Predetermined.asRoot_existsUnique`, `Predetermined.priceLevel_existsUnique_tree`, `Predetermined.priceLevel_homogeneous_tree`). |
| p. 158 | `ψ_p < 0` as a consequence of Assumption 3.1. | `ψ_p = Π₁₁ + Π₁₂`, and single-peakedness controls only `Π₁₁`. | (1.33), p. 160 | `log Y_t = log Yⁿ_t + (1 + κ)⁻¹(log 𝒴_t − E_{t−1}log 𝒴_t)`, and the matching price-level formula. | Each printed formula holds iff `log Yⁿ_t = E_{t−1}log Yⁿ_t` (`Predetermined.PC.printed_1_33_iff`, `Predetermined.PC.printed_price_level_iff`). Correct: the surprise is `(log 𝒴 − E log 𝒴) − (log Yⁿ − E log Yⁿ)`, with output `log Yⁿ + (1 + κ)⁻¹·surprise` and price level `E log 𝒴 − E log Yⁿ + κ(1 + κ)⁻¹·surprise` (`Predetermined.PC.output_corrected`, `Predetermined.PC.price_level_corrected`). Counterexample on the coin-flip tree: for every `κ > 0`, (1.32) holds at every date, but at date 1 the printed formula gives `log Y₁ = u₁` while the true value is `κu₁/(1 + κ)` (`Predetermined.printed_1_33_counterexample`). |
| B.1, p. 657 | The materials aggregate costs `P` per unit. | Exact for Dixit–Stiglitz; for Kimball only at symmetric prices, which is the case B.2 uses, also with a continuum of goods when all but a null set charge `P` (`Kimball.kimball_unit_cost_symmetric`, `Kimball.kimball_unit_cost_ae`). False in general: with `ψ(x) = (4x − x²)/3`, two goods of weight ½, `P = C = 1` and prices `(7/5, 1/5)`, the cost-minimising bundle `(3/5, 9/5)` satisfies (B.2)–(B.3) but costs `3/5 ≠ 1` (`Kimball.psiQ`, `Kimball.kimball_unit_cost_counterexample`). |
| B.3, p. 659 | The recursive form of (2.8) has `−ω̃θ log P_t`. | The sign is `+ω̃θ log P_t`, and only that version gives (B.13) (`Calvo.recursive_reset_price`, `Calvo.recursive_iff_B13`). Counterexample: under perfect foresight with `α = β = 1/2`, `ω̃θ = 1`, `log μ = 0`, `log S ≡ 0`, `log P ≡ 1`, the reset price `log p* ≡ 1/2` satisfies (2.8), but the printed right-hand side equals `−1/4` (`Calvo.printed_E4_fails`). |
| B.3, p. 660 | `(1 − λ₁)(1 − λ₂) = (1 − λ₁)(1 − βλ₁) = ξλ₁`. | `(1 − λ₁)(1 − λ₂)` is always negative, so it never equals `ξλ₁` (`QuadraticRoots.printed_E5_neg`, `QuadraticRoots.printed_E5_ne`), and the first step is also false (`QuadraticRoots.printed_E5_step_ne`). | (B.28), p. 667 | The profit gradient has the bracket `p − μs^j`. | The bracket is `p − μPs^j`, since `s^j` is real marginal cost; the printed value is not the derivative whenever `P ≠ 1` (`TwoSector.profit_gradient`, `TwoSector.printed_B28_not_derivative`). |
| (B.30), pp. 667–669 | `η` multiplies `(φ̂_j + Ŷⁿ − Ŷ_jⁿ)`. | The coefficient is `1/η`: `p̂_jⁿ = η⁻¹(φ̂_j + Ŷⁿ − Ŷ_jⁿ)` (`TwoSector.realMC_B30`). At `η = 2` the true derivative is 0 and the printed formula gives 1 (`TwoSector.printed_B30_fails`); with the printed `η` the display on p. 669 holds iff `(η² − 1)p̂_jⁿ = 0` (`TwoSector.printed_display_iff`). |

| p. 227 | `E₀ log Y_t = −(1 + ω_p)⁻¹w̃_t = (1 + ω_p)⁻¹λ^{t+1}`, as "(4.2) indicates". | The middle term is `(1 + ω_p)⁻¹(1 − w̃_t)`; the printed middle term never equals the right-hand side, differing by `(1 + ω_p)⁻¹` (`StickyWages.output_response`, `StickyWages.printed_output_response_ne`). `E₀ log Y` means the response of `log Y − log Yⁿ`, and the result follows from the `log Y` relation and (2.15), not from (4.2), which is labour demand. |

| Prop 3.4, p. 182; B.3 | A unique difference-stationary solution. | "Difference-stationary" is `\|E_t x_{t+j} − x_t\| ≤ Kj` uniformly in `t` and the state (`Calvo.DiffStationary`). | B.3, p. 660 | `lim E_tΔlog S_T = γ`, hence a finite bound `K`. | The limit does not give a bound uniform in `t` and the state. The proof uses bounded first differences of `log S`, which is assumed directly (`Calvo.BoundedIncr`, `Calvo.diffStationary_of_boundedIncr`, `Calvo.prop34`). |
| Prop 3.6, p. 190; B.5, p. 663 | The unique solution satisfying the terminal condition; "difference-stationary". | Deterministic: unique among bounded sequences, equivalently `p̃ → 1`, with the unbounded continuum `c(λ₂^{k+1} − λ₁^{k+1})` outside (`Persistence.prop_3_6_all_solutions`). Stochastic: unique among adapted solutions with `\|E_t log P_{t+j}\| ≤ A + B(t + j)`, a class containing every bounded-gap solution; outside it the explosive solutions are uncountable (`Persistence.nkpc_existsUnique`, `Persistence.linGrowth_of_gap`, `Persistence.nkpc_not_unique`, `Persistence.nkpc_solutions_not_countable`). |
| pp. 189, 197, 209 | The shocks are independent of `q`. | Not needed for the impulse responses: by linearity the `ε`-component of spending is `cỸ_{r−s}ε` whatever the other innovations, and the equilibrium is `p + cp̃_{r−s}ε`; only `E_rε = 0` for `r < s` is used (`SpendingGrowth.arGrowth_shock`, `Persistence.shock_response`). Independence cannot be stated for an abstract expectation operator; on an explicit two-coin event tree it is proved and the unique equilibrium has the Figure 3.12 property without a revision hypothesis (`Delays.shockTree_independent`, `Delays.fig_3_12_tree_equilibrium`). |

The difference is a constant and disappears in log deviations (`TwoSector.revenue_per_good`, `TwoSector.logdev_normalisation`). |
